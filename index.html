How to Get Better Streaming Performance

A stable internet connection, suitable video quality, and a working stream all affect playback. Even with a fast connection, a stream can buffer when the source server is overloaded or the feed is unavailable.

Check your internet connection

If a stream takes a long time to load, first check whether other websites and video services work normally. You can try switching between Wi-Fi and mobile data to identify whether the problem is specific to one connection.

For viewers using mobile data, keep an eye on data consumption. Live video can use a substantial amount of data, particularly at higher resolutions.

Choose a suitable video quality

If the app provides video-quality options, choose one that matches your connection. A lower resolution may provide more consistent playback on a slower network.

Higher resolution does not always mean a better viewing experience. A stream that pauses every few seconds can be less useful than a lower-resolution stream that plays steadily.

Close unnecessary background apps

Applications downloading large files, syncing photos, or playing other videos can compete for network bandwidth. Closing unnecessary activities may help when your connection is already limited.

It is also worth keeping enough free storage available and restarting your phone if the app becomes unresponsive.

Check whether the stream itself is available

If one channel fails while other internet services work correctly, the issue may involve the specific stream rather than your phone. Try another available channel or check whether the event is listed as live.

Repeatedly reinstalling the app will not fix an unavailable source stream. If the same problem affects every channel, check the app's compatibility and service status before making further changes.
